# Queue, waitlist, reservation, and public live-occupancy products for cafes and restaurants in Indonesia (as of October 2026), plus international analogues

Research date: 2026-10-13. Method note for the report writer: most findings below come from web-search result summaries of the linked pages, not from a full read of each page (only the Eat App comparison article was fetched in full; the DailySocial Zomato page failed to load with a certificate error). Several searches hit rate limits. Treat exact figures as "as reported by the linked page" and re-open the link before quoting a number in a deliverable. Items I could not source are listed under Gaps, not asserted.

## A1. What each queue / waitlist / reservation tool in Indonesia does, who uses it, merchant pricing, and city coverage (Yogyakarta or only Jakarta/Bali?)

### Takeaway
Every reservation platform I could confirm in Indonesia is positioned at Jakarta and Bali, at hotel F&B, fine dining, and tourist venues; none of the sources mention Yogyakarta. The consumer directory/reservation layer has thinned out (Zomato gone in 2023, Traveloka Eats discontinued, Chope absorbed by Grab in 2024), and I found no published IDR merchant price list for any of them.

### Cited Findings

- Grab acquired Chope's operations in Singapore, Indonesia, and Thailand, announced 22-23 July 2024; Hong Kong and China were excluded; terms undisclosed. — [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/grab-acquires-chopes-southeast-asian-businesses); [BNN Bloomberg](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/international/2024/07/23/grab-acquires-singapore-based-dining-reservation-firm-chope-bt)

**Chains known for long lines**

Haidilao Singapore offered QR online reservation at 6 of 12 outlets (2020). I found nothing describing Haidilao Indonesia's queue system. — [SAYS Malaysia](https://says.com/my/makan/haidilao-malaysia-introduces-online-queueing-system-so-you-don-t-have-to-wait-for-table); [SethLui, 2020](https://sethlui.com/?p=185423)
- Mie Gacoan: academic queue studies describe a single-cashier, first-come-first-served, multi-phase counter queue; proposed fixes are more cashiers or service stations, not an app. — [Universitas Malikussaleh, Binjai outlet](https://rama.unimal.ac.id/id/eprint/14107/2/ABSTRAK%20FIX.pdf); [Universitas Muhammadiyah Gresik](https://journal.umg.ac.id/index.php/justi/article/view/6718)
- Kopi Kenangan's app lets customers set a preferred pickup time; the store listing claims more than 800 outlets. - Fore Coffee's co-founder said in 2019 that most orders were made through its app (no percentage given; older than 2024). — [KrASIA, 2019](https://kr-asia.com/fore-coffee-announced-us8-5-million-funding-and-is-challenged-by-a-new-competitor)

### Inferences
- No source places any reservation or waitlist platform in Yogyakarta. Coverage statements are consistently "Jakarta and Bali"; absence of Yogyakarta is an absence of evidence across these sources, not a confirmed "not available".
- Reserve with Google in Indonesia, if live, is reachable only through an enterprise partner such as TableCheck, so it is effectively out of reach for an independent cafe that has no booking vendor.
- Chain coffee apps (Kopi Kenangan, Fore) solve waiting by pre-order and pickup, which removes the need for a seat. They are substitutes for takeaway demand, not for a customer who wants a table to sit and work.
- The Indonesian "queue app" category is mostly student or early-stage projects (Antria, Antreless, 7Queue) plus a general-purpose queue player (Qiwii) aimed at institutions; none shows evidence of scale in cafes.

### Gaps
- No IDR merchant price or commission rate found for Chope, TableCheck, Eatigo, Qiwii, Antrique, Qiscus, or Qontak/Mekari. Only USD list prices from a competitor's blog.
- Eatigo's current status in Indonesia (2024-2026) is unconfirmed: searches returned only 2019 launch coverage and no shutdown notice.
- Qraved's current status and whether it still takes reservations: not found.
- Antrique: no official page or status found, only a mention in a thesis.
- Qontak/Mekari and Kata.ai-style restaurant queue bots: no restaurant-specific queue product found; not confirmed either way.
- Haidilao Indonesia and Sushi Tei Indonesia queue mechanics: not found (two searches for Haidilao returned only Malaysia/Singapore; Sushi Tei was not searched separately).
- Google's own official partner and country list for Reserve with Google was not retrieved; the Indonesia claim rests on an undated TableCheck help article that conflicts with Appointy's list.

## A2. Is any of it aimed at or affordable for independent coffee shops and student-area cafes?

### Takeaway
Nothing found is aimed at independent or student-area cafes. The established products target hotels, fine dining, and tourist venues at USD subscription or commission pricing, and the only free option in practical use is the WhatsApp Business app, which has no queue function.

### Cited Findings
- Positioning of the main vendors: TableCheck for international hotel F&B, SevenRooms for luxury resorts (above US$500 per month), OpenTable for tourists at a few Bali venues (US$299-499 per month plus per-cover fees). Competitor source. — [Eat App blog, 2026](https://restaurant.eatapp.co/blog/best-restaurant-reservation-software-apps-in-indonesia-2026)
- The cheapest dedicated tools cited are Eat App's free tier (100 covers per month) and paid tier from about US$69 (about Rp1.1 million) per month, and Tableo's free entry tier; both are reservation/table tools billed in USD. Vendor marketing. — [Eat App blog, 2026](https://restaurant.eatapp.co/blog/best-restaurant-reservation-software-apps-in-indonesia-2026)
- WhatsApp Business app is free for small restaurants (up to 5 devices) but automation is limited to greeting and away messages. — [respond.io](https://respond.io/id/blog/whatsapp-business-for-restaurants)
- Existing Indonesian queue platforms were judged by a Telkom University thesis as not focused on the food-and-beverage or quick-service sector. — [Telkom University repository](https://repositori.telkomuniversity.ac.id/pustaka/files/216317/bab1/implementasi-reusable-component-user-interface-pada-aplikasi-web-manajemen-restoran-antria-dalam-bentuk-buku-karya-ilmiah.pdf)

### Inferences
- A cover-based reservation product does not fit a walk-in, order-at-counter coffee shop where guests stay for hours on one drink; the unit these tools price on (covers, bookings) is not the unit such a cafe manages (seats and tables over time).
- For a student-area cafe, about Rp1.1 million per month for a reservation tool is a material cost with unclear return, so the realistic competitor at this tier is "free WhatsApp plus nothing", not a paid platform.

### Gaps
- No vendor was found stating a product or price point for independent Indonesian coffee shops. I searched vendor comparison pages and Indonesian queue-app terms; POS table-management features were excluded by assignment and may contain the nearest affordable option.
- No data on how many Indonesian cafes take reservations at all.

## A3. How do busy cafes and restaurants in Indonesia handle waiting customers today?

### Takeaway
Reported practice is on-site and manual: a numbered waiting list with staff calling names or numbers, counter queues with paper-style queue numbers, and WhatsApp chat for bookings. I found no survey quantifying methods, and no evidence of a widely adopted virtual waitlist for cafes.

### Cited Findings
- Warkop Bulungan (Blok M, Jakarta): visitors register on a waiting list, get a sequence number, and are called by waiters about 10 minutes later. — [Mojok](https://mojok.co/terminal/warkop-bulungan-satu-lagi-tempat-viral-di-blok-m-yang-bikin-kecewa/amp/)
- Mie Gacoan: customers queue from noon to night with queue numbers in the hundreds, as cited in a Telkom University journal article. — [Telkom University journal](https://openlibrarypublications.telkomuniversity.ac.id/index.php/artdesign/article/download/25960/24761/51079)
- Mie Gacoan Binjai study: total waiting time of 8-35 minutes and an average of 10 people abandoning the queue (per the study's observation window). — [Universitas Malikussaleh](https://rama.unimal.ac.id/id/eprint/14107/2/ABSTRAK%20FIX.pdf)
- A February 2026 article describes long queues at viral eateries as increasingly normal and says the queue itself reinforces the venue's appeal. — [Media Asuransi News](https://mediaasuransinews.co.id/ekonomi/fenomena-antre-di-tempat-makan-viral-worth-it-atau-sekadar-ikut-tren/)
- WhatsApp is described as the channel Indonesian guests actually use to book, with no system behind it. Competitor-vendor framing. — [Eat App blog, 2026](https://restaurant.eatapp.co/blog/best-restaurant-reservation-software-apps-in-indonesia-2026)
- Many Yogyakarta cafes already apply "order first, then sit", and owners complain about groups where only a few order while others use the facilities. — [Mojok](https://mojok.co/terminal/siasat-kafe-menghadapi-pelanggan-mahasiswa-yang-meresahkan/)
- Indonesian academic prototypes for restaurant table queuing by SMS date back to 2013, and WhatsApp queue-number notification has been prototyped for a community health centre. — [Jurnal TESLA UNTAR](https://journal.untar.ac.id/index.php/tesla/article/view/323/266); [STMIK DCI journal](https://jurnal.stmik-dci.ac.id/index.php/jumika/article/download/1231/815)

### Inferences
- The virtual waitlist looks like an open gap in product terms (no adopted tool found), but the evidence also suggests weak merchant pull: a visible queue is treated as marketing for viral venues, and the documented cafe pain in Yogyakarta is long-staying low-spend guests, not lost walk-ins.
- Queue abandonment is observed (Binjai) but only in a single small study of a fast-casual chain; it is not evidence about cafes.

### Gaps
- No survey or industry report on waiting-customer handling in Indonesian cafes was found. Evidence is anecdotal (opinion columns, single-outlet student studies).
- No Yogyakarta-specific reporting on cafe waitlists was found.
- No academic study on queue abandonment or occupancy display effects specific to Indonesia was retrieved; the general international literature was not searched in depth.

## B1. Public live-occupancy products: data source, business model, who pays, survival

### Takeaway
The products that show the public how full a place is fall into three data models: phone location (Google), sensors or cameras paid for by the venue or landlord (VACAN, Occuspace/Waitz), and staff or queue-system input (VACAN's button, Korea's Catchtable/Tabling, China's Meituan/Dianping queue). All confirmed survivors are paid by the venue, facility owner, or a platform with another revenue line; none found operates in Indonesia except Google.

### Cited Findings

**Google Maps Popular Times and Live busyness (operates in Indonesia as part of Google Maps)**
- Computed from aggregated, anonymised data from users who opted in to Google Location History/Timeline, which is off by default. — [Google Maps help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/11323117?hl=en)
- Popular times show typical busyness per hour of the week relative to the venue's busiest hour, based on average popularity over the last few months; live busyness is overlaid in real time. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products/maps/maps101-popular-times-and-live-busyness-information/); [Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6263531?hl=en)
- Businesses cannot enter the data themselves, and it appears only if Google has sufficient visit data for the business; where data is insufficient, nothing is published. — [Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6263531?hl=en)
- Criticism (single third-party explainer, anecdotal): with few opted-in users live data can be inaccurate, more so when a business sits close to others, and the system counts devices rather than people. — [Technipages](https://www.technipages.com/how-does-google-know-if-a-place-is-busy)
- Validation evidence is indirect: a 2020 study found similarity between Wi-Fi device counts and Popular Times "relatively promising"; a tourism study of 198 restaurants found the data matched theoretical expectations; a Semarang, Indonesia study of road segments found no significant difference in peak times but discrepancies of -60 to +50 minutes. — [Smart Cities (MDPI), 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-6511/3/3/42/xml); [Tourism Review (Emerald)](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/TR-10-2018-0152/full/html); [Universitas Diponegoro journal](https://ejournal.undip.ac.id/index.php/mkts/article/view/79549)

**VACAN (Japan; no evidence of operation in Indonesia)**
- Uses IoT sensors, cameras, and AI to detect vacancy at restaurant floors, cafes, toilets, tourist sites, evacuation shelters, and polling stations, distributed to signage and smartphones; products include VACAN Maps. — [Dealroom profile](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/vacan); [Yourator profile](https://www.yourator.co/companies/VACAN?locale=en)
- For eateries, vacancy can be sent either by staff pressing a button-type device in the shop or by automatic judgement from camera data. Reported in 2020 Japanese tech press; the exact page among the search results was not pinned down. — [Impress Watch](https://www.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1251393.html); [ASCII](https://ascii.jp/elem/000/004/020/4020717/)
- Revenue is B2B: data as a service to commercial facilities, office buildings, hotels, and more than 200 Japanese municipalities (profile-site claim). — [Dealroom profile](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/vacan)
- In 2020 VACAN offered the service free for a period (until 30 October 2020 in Kyushu) and free trials for Osaka shopping streets. — [ReseMom/PR Times, 2020](https://resemom.jp/release/prtimes/20200522/62668.html)
- Still active: raised venture funding in February 2024 from Wing Capital Partners, Japanet Holdings, Pegasus Tech Ventures, and Yamanashi Chugin Management Consulting. — [Preqin](https://www.preqin.com/data/profile/asset/vacan--inc-/330886)

**Korea: Catchtable, Tabling, Naver (no evidence of operation in Indonesia)**
- Catchtable was founded in 2018 as a B2B restaurant tool and launched consumer real-time reservation in 2020; by mid-2023 it reported over 3 million monthly active users, more than 7,000 affiliated stores, and over 650,000 waits handled by its waiting service. — [KED Global, July 2023](https://www.kedglobal.com/us/korean-startups/newsView/ked202307140003)
- Remote waitlisting depends on the restaurant; some pages allow only on-site waiting. — [Korea Decoded guide](https://koreadecoded.duckdns.org/korea-restaurant-waitlist-apps-catchtable-tabling/)
- Tabling is described as a major app for remote line-up, reservations, and takeout, seen on tablets at restaurant entrances (single blog source). — [Korea Experience, 2026](https://koreaexperience.com/blog/tabling-app-for-waitlists-2026)
- Deposits and cancellation policy on Catchtable and Naver Reservation are set per restaurant; Korea's Fair Trade Commission criteria effective 18 December 2025 cap no-show fees at 40% of the bill for reservation-based restaurants and 20% for general restaurants. — [SeoulStart guide](https://seoulstart.com/guides/catchtable-reservations-guide)

**China: Meituan / Dianping queue (no evidence of operation in Indonesia)**
- Consumers tap the queue button on a restaurant page, choose party size, receive a digital number, and watch tables-ahead count down in real time. — [Wander in China guide, 2026](https://wanderinchina.com/survival-guide/useful-mobile-apps/dazhong-dianping-meituan/)
- The merchant-side Meituan Queue app lets staff set table types, call numbers, skip no-shows, and mark seated; basic functions are stated as free and the queue entry can surface on Meituan and Dianping. App-download listing, unverified vendor claim. — [32r.com listing](https://www.32r.com/app/89887.html)

**Library and campus seat trackers: Occuspace / Waitz (US; no evidence of operation in Indonesia)**
- Plug-in sensors detect Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Low Energy signals from nearby devices; machine learning estimates headcount; no cameras or infrared. Output appears in the free Waitz app, on entrance monitors, and on university pages. — [Campus Technology, Nov 2022](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2022/11/17/occuspace-expands-occupancy-monitoring-to-more-colleges-and-universities.aspx?p=1); [University Business](https://universitybusiness.com/how-sensors-are-helping-colleges-monitor-occupancy/)
- Accuracy is company-claimed at 90% (93% per the CEO in a student paper); UCSB library staff occasionally validate with manual counts. — [Campus Technology, Nov 2022](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2022/11/17/occuspace-expands-occupancy-monitoring-to-more-colleges-and-universities.aspx?p=1); [Daily Nexus](https://dailynexus.com/?p=113216)
- The institution pays: approximately US$600 per year for under 5,000 square feet, one sensor per 2,500-5,000 square feet (2022 figure, older than 2024). — [Campus Technology, Nov 2022](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2022/11/17/occuspace-expands-occupancy-monitoring-to-more-colleges-and-universities.aspx?p=1)

**Indonesia: live cafe occupancy**
- No operating Indonesian product showing live cafe occupancy or free tables to the public was found. What surfaced are academic or student works: a Telkom University cafe-reservation app design for Bandung motivated by not knowing a cafe's condition before arriving (prototype); a Yogyakarta web app for finding cafes with hotspots (facilities and reviews, no occupancy); and DINE, an ITS student app from the pandemic with reservation, QR code, and visitor-capacity limiting. — [Telkom University journal](https://openlibrarypublications.telkomuniversity.ac.id/index.php/artdesign/article/download/12968/13385/26269); [UTDI Yogyakarta thesis](https://eprints.utdi.ac.id/3947/2/2_115410082_BAB_I.pdf.pdf); [ITS News](https://www.its.ac.id/news/lewat-dine-mahasiswa-its-bantu-pemesanan-dine-in-di-masa-pandemi/)

### Inferences
- where2go's model (staff report free tables, table by table, shown publicly as numbers) has one clear precedent in VACAN's staff-button mode, and a structural cousin in queue systems where staff seat and call parties. No precedent found pairs staff-reported free-table counts with a public cross-venue ranking in Indonesia.
- Google is the only substitute present in Yogyakarta. Its limits are structural for this use case: it reports relative busyness, not free tables; it needs enough opted-in visitors, which small cafes may lack; and the merchant cannot correct it.
- In every surviving case found, the payer is not the consumer: facility owners and municipalities (VACAN), universities (Occuspace), or a platform monetising reservations, deposits, and merchant tools (Catchtable, Meituan).

### Gaps
- Apple Maps busyness, Starbucks or other chain store-busyness indicators, Naver real-time waiting specifics, and Taiwan/Singapore equivalents were not researched (search budget and rate limits). No claims are made about them.
- Google live-busyness coverage for small cafes in Indonesian cities: no source found; Google only states that data shows when visit volume is sufficient.
- VACAN's restaurant pricing and how many independent eateries (as opposed to malls, stations, municipalities) still use it: not found. Founder/CEO naming also conflicts between profile sites (Dealroom vs Preqin/Diamond), so it is omitted.
- Merchant fees for Catchtable and Tabling: not found.

## B2. Evidence on why merchants do or do not take part, especially busy ones

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on merchant motivation is thin. What exists points to three frictions: busy venues benefit from a visible queue, remote queueing invites abuse and gets switched off, and vendors had to give the service away to get eateries on board.

### Cited Findings
- Queues at viral Indonesian eateries are described as reinforcing the venue's appeal, since people want to join what is being talked about. — [Media Asuransi News, Feb 2026](https://mediaasuransinews.co.id/ekonomi/fenomena-antre-di-tempat-makan-viral-worth-it-atau-sekadar-ikut-tren/)
- In China, some restaurants that had enabled phone queueing later turned it off, with their Dianping page stating in-person queueing is required; a sushi chain reportedly faced resale of queue numbers at 25-60 yuan (up to 120 yuan) and responded with phone-number and SIM verification. From a search summary of a Chinese news item; open the page before quoting. — [Sichuan Online (scol.com.cn)](https://cbgc.scol.com.cn/news/7436230)
- Catchtable notes remote waitlisting is a per-restaurant choice, and some restaurants allow on-site waiting only. — [Korea Decoded guide](https://koreadecoded.duckdns.org/korea-restaurant-waitlist-apps-catchtable-tabling/)
- VACAN ran free-of-charge periods and free trials for eateries and shopping streets in 2020. — [ReseMom/PR Times, 2020](https://resemom.jp/release/prtimes/20200522/62668.html)
- Yogyakarta cafe staff describe their problem as students who stay for hours, come in groups, and order little; suggested countermeasures include order-before-sitting. — [Mojok](https://mojok.co/terminal/siasat-kafe-menghadapi-pelanggan-mahasiswa-yang-meresahkan/)
- Google gives merchants no control: busyness cannot be entered or edited by the business. — [Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6263531?hl=en)

### Inferences
- A cafe that is already full gains little from advertising that it is full, and may fear that showing "0 free tables" diverts demand. The stronger merchant case is for cafes with spare capacity (showing free tables pulls walk-ins) and for off-peak hours.
- Publishing free tables to students could worsen the long-stay, low-spend problem owners already complain about, unless the product also carries the cafe's rules (minimum order, time limits), which where2go's detail page already does.
- Staff input is the cheapest data source but depends on a busy barista updating it; VACAN offering both a one-press button and camera automation suggests manual input alone was a known adoption risk.

### Gaps
- No interview, survey, or case study with Indonesian cafe owners about sharing occupancy publicly was found.
- No data on staff-input compliance or data freshness in VACAN's button mode.
- No quantified effect of occupancy display on merchant revenue or walk-ins was retrieved.

## B3. Evidence of consumer demand in Indonesia for knowing cafe crowdedness before going

### Takeaway
Demand is implied rather than measured: WFC and student study-cafe culture in Yogyakarta is heavily covered, long stays are documented, and at least one academic project names "not knowing the cafe's condition before arriving" as the problem. No survey quantifying the need was found.

### Cited Findings
- Mainstream outlets publish lists of WFC cafes in Yogyakarta, including 24-hour venues, signalling sustained search interest in where to work from a cafe. — [Liputan6](https://www.liputan6.com/lifestyle/read/6224243/26-rekomendasi-cafe-untuk-wfc-di-jogja-fasilitas-lengkap-dan-buka-24-jam); [JawaPos](https://www.jawapos.com/kuliner/2605260281/10-kafe-buku-di-jogja-yang-nyaman-untuk-mahasiswa-nugas-wifi-kencang-dan-bikin-produktif)
- Cafes in Demangan Baru (near Gejayan) are described as filled mostly with visitors there to finish assignments. — [Mojok](https://mojok.co/terminal/3-kafe-di-demangan-baru-jogja-yang-nyaman-buat-nugas/)
- Visitors at one Yogyakarta book cafe commonly stay more than three hours. — [JawaPos](https://www.jawapos.com/kuliner/2605260281/10-kafe-buku-di-jogja-yang-nyaman-untuk-mahasiswa-nugas-wifi-kencang-dan-bikin-produktif)
- A Telkom University design study states that the condition of a cafe is often unknown before people arrive, and proposes an information platform (prototype only). — [Telkom University journal](https://openlibrarypublications.telkomuniversity.ac.id/index.php/artdesign/article/download/12968/13385/26269)
- A Yogyakarta student project built a web app to find cafes with hotspot facilities, showing the selection problem is framed around facilities, not crowding. — [UTDI Yogyakarta thesis](https://eprints.utdi.ac.id/3947/2/2_115410082_BAB_I.pdf.pdf)

### Inferences
- Long dwell times mean seat turnover is slow, so a cafe that is full at 19:00 likely stays full; that makes "is there a free table now" a more useful signal for this audience than Google's relative busyness curve.
- Existing consumer content answers "which cafe is good for working" (Wi-Fi, sockets, hours), not "which one has a table right now". That is consistent with an unserved need, but equally consistent with users coping by habit or by messaging friends.

### Gaps
- No survey, poll, or quantitative study of Indonesian consumers wanting cafe crowdedness information was found.
- Social-media evidence (TikTok, X, Instagram posts about full cafes in Yogyakarta) was not retrievable through the search tool used.
- No evidence found on willingness to switch cafes based on a live free-table number.
